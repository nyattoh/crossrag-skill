# Geometry and methodological boundaries

The baseline `engine.py` evaluates candidate utterances/items from a user-supplied conversation or corpus. For each item, its full formatted text (`aとb。line`) is embedded, and its concept pair `(a, b)` is embedded separately.

Each retrieval mode in `engine.py` / `run.py` ranks candidates strictly by a **single mathematical metric** (`rank_value`), with **no composite score, cosine-band target, novelty weight, or coherence weight**:

- **similar** (`ranking_metric: "cosine"`): cosine similarity between the normalised average of context anchors and the candidate text vector (`cosine(ctx, vec)`).
- **orthogonal** (`ranking_metric: "orthogonal_residual"`): Euclidean norm of the projection residual from the linear subspace spanned by the context anchors (`||v - P_span(anchors)(v)||`). No band, novelty, or coherence weights are mixed in.
- **wedge** (`ranking_metric: "gram_bivector_cos"`): oriented bivector cosine between `a∧b` (first two context terms) and `u∧v` (candidate concept pair), computed via the Gram determinant `((a·u)(b·v)-(a·v)(b·u)) / (||a∧b|| ||u∧v||)` without materialising `d×d` matrices. The *first two* context terms determine orientation. Swapping term order flips the sign; this does NOT entail a natural linguistic direction.

Each returned row exposes `rank_value` (equal to the mode's single metric), `ranking_metric`, and the three raw geometric quantities `cosine`, `orthogonal`, and `wedge`. The legacy blended `score` field and `random` mode are removed from `engine.py` and `run.py`.

## Corpus source and encoder transparency

- **No implicit demo corpus**: `engine.run()` raises `ValueError` when `corpus=None` unless `use_demo_corpus=True` is explicitly passed. `run.py` requires `--conversation <path>`, `--corpus <path>`, or explicit `--demo-corpus`. The bundled 22-item `DEFAULT_CORPUS` and `references/example-corpus.json` are strictly for explicit demonstration/smoke tests and must never be used implicitly.
- **Conversation-derived candidates**: Normal operation takes conversation data (`--conversation` or user conversation utterances) and records `corpus_source` (`"conversation"`, `"explicit"`, or `"demo"`).
- **Encoder disclosure**: Output always records `encoder`. `HashEncoder` (`hash-ngram（文字特徴・意味理解ではない）`) is a reproducible character n-gram hash representation, **not** a semantic embedding model. For semantic embeddings, use `--encoder semantic` with a local `SentenceTransformer` model (e.g. `intfloat/multilingual-e5-small`).

A wedge or residual computation is pure vector geometry; it does not decode text, discover hidden concepts, or prove factual claims. By default, report only the raw mathematical calculation results without adding unrequested interpretation.

## Experimental normal-ray mode

The experimental `normal-ray` mode defaults to `--ray-method residual-cluster` and requires concepts A, B, an explicit source article C, and a corpus. A, B, C and every corpus candidate must be embedded in one shared coordinate space: all are encoded with the single `encoder.encode_passage` path, using A, B and C as input text and each candidate as its formatted corpus-row text (for example, `aとb。line` for pair rows). With role-prefixed encoders, any known leading `query: ` or `passage: ` marker is removed before encoding, then the encoder applies the same passage prefix to every vector; prefix-free encoders add no role prefix. Original corpus text is retained in hit provenance. `encode_query` is never used in this mode. Use one encoder, one encoding path, and one prefix policy for all vectors. Let `a`, `b`, and `c` be their resulting raw coordinates. The affine source plane is `S = c + span(a-c, b-c)`; its ray origin is `c`. With `V = span(a-c, b-c)`, each corpus passage vector `x` has residual `r(x) = (x-c) - P_V(x-c)`, orthogonal to `V`. Coordinates are not centred; the residual is translation invariant. Residuals with norm at most `min_residual_norm` (default `1e-9`) are treated as in-plane: their axial distance is set exactly to `0.0`, their radial distance is therefore `||x-c||`, and they are unusable for clustering but remain eligible for retrieval geometry. This threshold defines in-plane membership, so even `0 < ||r|| <= min_residual_norm` counts as in-plane; a start distance below that scale (for example, `1e-10`) can differ from the exact mathematical `t`. For example, exact duplicate embeddings of A, B or C are reported with `--ray-start-distance 0` when their radial distance is at most `--ray-radius`, and never with the default start distance `0.25`.

Usable passages are processed in original corpus-index order by deterministic leader clustering: the lowest unassigned index seeds a cluster, and each later unassigned passage joins when its residual cosine to the seed direction is at least `--ray-cluster-cosine` (default `0.7`, range `(0,1]`), with a floating-point tolerance of `1e-12`; thus `--ray-cluster-cosine 1` groups numerically parallel residuals. Membership is order-dependent by design but reproducible; thresholds are uncalibrated across encoders. Clusters smaller than `--ray-min-cluster-size` (default `2`) produce no ray. A direction is the normalised arithmetic mean of member raw residuals. The output records `mean_residual`, `resultant_norm`, and `coherence = ||sum r|| / sum ||r||`. A zero resultant yields no ray and increments `clustering.zero_resultant_cluster_count`.

For every non-excluded corpus passage, clustered or not, set `u = x-c`, `t = u·n`, and full-space `radial = ||u - t n||`. A passage is reported when `t >= --ray-start-distance` (default `0.25`) and `radial <= --ray-radius` (default `0.25`), inclusively. Forward and radial tests compare floating-point values exactly at their inclusive boundaries, so a row whose true `t` or radial distance equals a threshold may fall on either side because of rounding. Near-zero residuals are treated as in-plane when their norm is at most `min_residual_norm`: their axial distance is set exactly to `0.0` and radial distance is `||x-c||`. Thus exact duplicate embeddings of A, B or C are reported with `--ray-start-distance 0` when radial distance is at most `--ray-radius`, and never with the default start distance `0.25`. This threshold defines in-plane membership, so even `0 < ||r|| <= min_residual_norm` counts as in-plane; a start distance below that scale (for example, `1e-10`) can differ from the exact mathematical `t`. The absolute `min_residual_norm` threshold assumes roughly unit-scale embeddings, as with the bundled hash and normalised encoders; at very large coordinate scales, rounding in an in-plane row's residual can exceed it, so the in-plane rule may not apply. The rule for text identical to A, B or C also assumes the encoder returns bit-identical vectors for identical text; this has not been verified for semantic encoders such as E5. Hits sort by radial distance then corpus index; one passage may occur under multiple rays. `in_cluster` indicates membership in that ray's defining cluster. Cluster members need not be near the ray in full-space distance: their component within plane S increases `radial`, and clustering does not constrain it. `--limit` caps rays by descending member count, then ascending seed index; `truncated_ray_count` counts the rest.

Residual-cluster performs no content or similarity pruning. Only explicit `--exclude` terms remove rows, using normalised substring matching over row values; `clustering.excluded_count` counts only these rows. Lexically or semantically close passages, including C itself if present, may be clustered or reported when geometry allows. Near-zero residuals are unusable only for clustering, not retrieval. For `s1 >= s2`, the singular values of `[a-c; b-c]`, the plane is unavailable when the embedding dimension is below 3 (`embedding_dimension_below_three`), when `s1 <= 1e-12` (`source_anchors_coincide`), or when `s2 <= 1e-6 * s1` (`plane_rank_deficient`). Non-finite or non-representable anchors raise a validation error (exit 2) rather than a plane status. No meaning, similarity, ranking, or relevance is scored: unrelated passages can be valid hits, no items are generated, and no hit yields an empty result. Statuses are `hits_found`, `no_hits` (rays exist but no hits), `no_residual_clusters` (no corpus rows remain after `--exclude`, no usable residuals, no cluster reaches `--ray-min-cluster-size`, or only zero-resultant clusters remain; the last case is possible only when `--ray-cluster-cosine` is about `1e-12` or smaller, given the `1e-12` tolerance), and `plane_unavailable` with reason `embedding_dimension_below_three`, `source_anchors_coincide`, or `plane_rank_deficient`.

The output contains `mode: "normal-ray"`, `method: "affine_residual_cluster"`, `input{A,B,C}`, `params`, `plane{origin,basis,singular_values}`, `clustering` counts and `unusable_corpus_indices`, `rays[{ray_id, seed_corpus_index, member_corpus_indices, member_count, direction, mean_residual, resultant_norm, coherence, hit_count, hits[{corpus_index,a,b,line,axial_distance,radial_distance,in_cluster}]}]`, `warnings`, and `disclaimer`. Indices are original corpus row order. In residual-cluster mode, `--no-center`, `--vocab`, `--ray-cluster-gap`, and `--ray-min-set-size` are rejected with exit code 2; `--C`, `--ray-cluster-cosine`, and `--ray-min-cluster-size` are rejected by `pca-cross` with exit code 2. The output records `params.encoding_path = "encode_passage"`. Distances are not calibrated across encoders. The direction is estimated from the supplied corpus distribution, not from an A×B cross product; it is not a semantic fact, and usefulness has not been evaluated.

Legacy method: `--ray-method pca-cross` retains the former centred PCA frame and A×B cross-product ray, including its old exclusion behaviour. Use it to reproduce earlier calls without C. The PCA method and its thresholds have not been evaluated for usefulness.
