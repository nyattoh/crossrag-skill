# Geometry and methodological boundaries

The baseline `engine.py` is copied from CrossRAG Lab v0.1. For each corpus item, its candidate full sentence is embedded; its concept pair is embedded separately.

- **similar**: cosine between averaged context anchors and candidate text.
- **orthogonal**: projection residual from the span of context anchors, blended with cosine-band and coherence weights.
- **wedge**: cosine of oriented bivectors `a∧b` and `u∧v` computed without materialising d-by-d arrays: `((a·u)(b·v)-(a·v)(b·u)) / (||a∧b|| ||u∧v||)` for unit vectors. The *first two* context terms determine orientation. Swapping term order flips the sign; this does NOT entail a natural linguistic direction.
- **random**: deterministic hash-based ranking used as a control.

These ranking formulae are **heuristics** with no established relation to story quality. HashEncoder is a reproducible character n-gram representation, not a semantic model. For actual semantic tests use a compatible embedding model (e.g. multilingual E5) and observe its query/passage conventions: the copied v0.1 prototype encodes strings symmetrically without such prefixes, so production-quality retrieval is not established. Model downloads are optional.

A wedge compares oriented planes; it does not itself decode text or discover a hidden concept. The LLM consumes the retrieved corpus motifs and writes the final text. Factual questions require separate evidence sources; use this tool to propose ideas, not certify them.

The experimental `normal-ray` mode selects one normal by fitting three global PCA components to corpus passage vectors centred on their own mean. Let `Q` be the orthonormal matrix whose rows are those components; let `a` and `b` be A and B after subtracting the corpus passage mean (or the embedding origin with `--no-center`). It computes `n = Q.T @ normalize((Q @ a) × (Q @ b))`. Whenever a ray is available, the lifted direction is orthogonal to both centred anchors; availability checks reject weak or collinear projections. The ray starts at `p = (a + b) / 2`; each candidate is centred by the same passage mean before measuring distance to `p`. The canonical PCA basis plus the ordered cross product `A × B` defines the sign convention; swapping A and B reverses the ray. Near-tied PCA sign pivots, leading components, and third/fourth components produce stability warnings, but do not guarantee orientation stability under corpus changes. This convention selects one ray from the full orthogonal complement and is not an intrinsic normal determined by A and B alone.

For each candidate `x`, it measures axial position `t = (x - p) · n` and radial distance `r = ||x - p - t n||`. Only candidates with `t >= --ray-start-distance` and `r <= --ray-radius` are considered; adjacent axial positions are joined when their gap is at most `--ray-cluster-gap`. Clusters smaller than `--ray-min-set-size` are omitted and `--limit` caps returned sets. A low-rank corpus frame or weak/collinear anchor projection returns `ray_unavailable`; no matching concepts returns `no_sets`. Both are valid outcomes. The PCA frame is fit on every passage before `--exclude` filters the returned candidates. Query and passage encoders may apply different prefixes, so candidate coordinates may not align exactly for such models; the output retains `pca_center` to make the applied coordinate transform inspectable. This is a map of corpus geometry, not evidence that the returned concepts are semantically related.
